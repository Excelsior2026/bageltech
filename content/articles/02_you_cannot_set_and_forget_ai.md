---
title: "You Cannot Set and Forget AI"
date: "2026-02-01"
author: "William Parris"
summary: "In consequential systems, deployment is the start of governance, not the end. AI systems drift, and so do the institutions around them."
---

One of the most dangerous ideas in enterprise AI is that once a model is deployed, tuned, and placed behind a policy document, the hard governance work is finished. It is not. In consequential systems, deployment is the start of governance, not the end.

AI systems drift in more than one way. Models drift as data changes. But institutions drift too. Policies evolve, operators change, exceptions accumulate, workarounds harden into routine practice, and the people who once understood why the system was built a certain way leave the organization. A system can remain technically online while becoming institutionally ungoverned.

That is why "set it and forget it" fails in two directions at once. First, the model may behave differently under new conditions than it did during evaluation. Second, the surrounding organization may lose the ability to recognize that difference, explain it, or correct it. In both cases, surface functionality can mask deep degradation.

We have seen this before in legacy technology transitions. Systems do not simply store data. They carry exception paths, tacit workflows, embedded judgment, and institutional memory. AI systems do the same, but with an added problem: they often conceal uncertainty and degrade gracefully rather than failing loudly. So the organization does not experience a clean break. It experiences a slow loss of visibility.

Responsible governance therefore requires standing practices, not one-time review. You need recurring audits, documented operating assumptions, explicit thresholds for revalidation, clear accountability when behavior changes, and a preserved history of why key decisions were made. Most of all, you need mechanisms that force the system to tell the truth when conditions have changed.

A system that must never be revisited is not a governed system. It is a frozen hope.

The real question is not whether your AI worked at launch. It is whether your organization will still understand what it is doing six months later, after the assumptions that once made it look safe have quietly moved underneath it.
